AI is one word for three jobs.

Ask which kind they have already encountered this month. A score, an alert, and a chatbot are all called AI — that sentence is on the canvas. Left box: PEWS and a febrile-infant pathway. Middle: sepsis alert and a chest X-ray read. Right: a parent letter and an AI scribe. FDA does not regulate “AI as such”; it regulates devices by intended use.

A model that is accurate on adults can still fail on children.

Distribution shift. Vendors often quote AUROC; say the model was accurate on adults so they hear the population, not the metric. The 6% PAS number is a conference abstract. Carry it as a prior, not as a prevalence study.

How AI answers

A dose is several tokens, not one number.

A dose is several tokens. That is why numeric hallucination is easy and why “just one number” is not a simple object inside the net. The split on the canvas is a cartoon and varies by model. Say that, then move on.

The model guesses the next token. It is not looking up an answer.

Spoken takeaway: it predicts plausible text; it is not retrieving a stored answer. Decoder-style LLM: given the tokens so far, score the next one, add it, repeat.

The same 38.5 °C is a workup at 3 weeks and often watch-and-treat at 3 years.

This replaces transformer anatomy. 38.5 °C in a 3-week-old is a workup clock. In a 3-year-old it is often watch-and-treat. Models need that context the same way you do. If they ask how attention works, the backup slide is after Q&A.

The usual mistake now is a real paper with the wrong claim.

Old failure: invented PubMed ID. Usual failure now: a real paper with the wrong claim; a rule set at the start can vanish mid-run; citation links that look like proof. Citations and tool use do not eliminate source verification. Artsi: interpretive error even with real citations.

The jobs to remember are autocomplete, chat, and tools.

The goal is a taxonomy they can keep when the posters change. Autocomplete finishes the next word. Chat is a conversation. Tools look things up or test a result. Product names change quickly; these three jobs are what to remember. Full chronology is in backup if someone wants GPT-2 through Fable.

Sources, limits, and files

Three ways to make an answer easier to inspect.

RAG looks a corpus up, then writes. Gemini Notebook is the best-known example.

Spell RAG once: retrieval-augmented generation — they have heard the acronym. Point at the corpus card — that is the choice. Gemini Notebook is the best-known example (public PDFs with citation links). Do not claim a measured market share. OpenEvidence and UpToDate are other corpora. Reduces some hallucinations. Does not stop wrong-paper, unsupported synthesis, leftover parametric claims, or citation theater.

These models can test an answer and revise it before responding.

The 400 mg maximum is the safety limit. 500 mg exceeds it, so the model revises to 400 mg.

Vendors sell extra tokens before the answer — a try, test, revise loop you usually do not see. Left is the 2023 chatbot: first fluent sentence, stop there. Right is o1-class: hidden scratch work, then one answer. The toy is a 10 mg/kg dose that needs a 400 mg maximum: 50 kg × 10 = 500, which fails the maximum, so the revised answer is 400 mg. The line under the boxes is that in English. Coding tests had an automated limit test like that first; keep that history off the canvas unless someone asks. Files and tools are the next slide. o1 Sep 2024; DeepSeek-R1 Jan 2025 if someone asks whether it was OpenAI-only.

A chatbot returns a paragraph. A harness returns a file.

Ask: which row produces something another person can inspect tomorrow? Top row is a chatbot: prompt in, a paragraph out, you copy it. Bottom row is Codex: prompt, files, tools, an artifact you can open, then a test. Demonstrations 1, 2, 5, and 6 make the file itself part of the evidence.

A high board score does not mean the model can handle a real patient.

The exam is comparable across models, and many of the exam questions are in the training data.

The context of a specific patient, in a specific hospital, and with a specific set of preferences and limitations, will likely always be a challenging application for models.

Board-style exams and vendor tables are comparable across models, and many of those items are already in the training data. A real case is a specific patient, in a specific hospital, with a specific set of preferences and limitations — language and cultural context included. That will likely always be a hard job for these models. A high board score does not mean the model can handle weight-based dosing. FDA 2026 paper is still asking how to test open-ended generative AI.

Evidence

For real notes, use a hospital-approved cloud or this laptop.

Three places a prompt can live. Consumer ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini send the prompt off-site with no hospital privacy contract. A hospital tool with a business associate agreement is the official cloud path. Laptop Ollama: the vendor never sees the prompt, and they already use that laptop for the EHR. Qwen3.8, Muse Glimmer, and Gemma 4 are examples of local model families, not endorsements or claims that they fit every laptop.

OpenEvidence is a search assistant, not a pediatric guideline.

Artsi 2026 OpenEvidence systematic review. 11 studies, none synthesized as pediatric. The appropriate use they found is guideline-style look-up; it often reinforces rather than changes a plan, and is weaker on complex cases. Not for acting without opening the source. The gray line is the vendor gap: the About page does not name a base model, say whether this is a fine-tuned medical model or a general model with retrieval, or publish a model card. They do advertise in-chat clinical calculators and DeepConsult, a literature agent. Public pages do not describe a coding sandbox. Do not invent LoRA or a GPT version from blogs. End on: search assistant, not pediatric guideline. Company “40% of US physicians” is vendor-reported. Registry: PROSPERO CRD420261289103. Do not invent a pediatric evidence base from 11 adult-leaning studies.

A real citation can still be the wrong answer.

Hajj inside Artsi: same 15 adult transcatheter tricuspid questions. Bars are the share scored fully accurate by the study’s own definition: ChatGPT-4o 10 of 15 (66.7%) vs OpenEvidence 4 of 15 (26.7%). n=15 is the whole study — gray footer, not a headline. OpenEvidence still cited papers; those citations did not make the answers correct. Do not present it as pediatric. Heuristic: OE for journals, UpToDate for the editorial corpus you already pay for, neither for “what do I do without looking.”

You cannot list a chatbot as an author. AAP has not written a pediatric AI policy.

As of August 2026. Left column is already in writing: ICMJE January 2026 and COPE 2023 — authors have to be humans who can take responsibility; a chatbot cannot be an author. AMA House of Delegates 10 June 2026 — AI must not replace physician judgment. Right column is what pediatrics still does not have: AAP’s January 2026 digital-ecosystems policy says a dedicated clinical AI statement is forthcoming, so do not invent one; the FDA August 2026 discussion paper is still asking how to test open-ended generative tools; Artsi reviewed 11 OpenEvidence studies and none were synthesized as pediatric. Three ways a model is taught: labels, structure, or a score.

Forty-five seconds if you use it at all. Supervised: x goes with y. Unsupervised: structure, no labels. Reinforcement is a roundabout: try, get scored, nudge, try again. Unlikely to change what they do tomorrow.

Attention is the mix step before the next-token guess.

Self-attention, 2017. One token looking at the sentence. The main lecture uses the 38.5 °C age example instead.

ChatGPT was the public rupture. o1 made thinking time a product.

Full chronology. GPT-2 unsupervised. GPT-3 in-context. InstructGPT RLHF. ChatGPT Nov 2022 is the public rupture. Llama 2 is why a laptop path exists. o1 Sep 2024 is test-time compute as a product. DeepSeek-R1 Jan 2025 showed reasoning-style RL was not OpenAI-only. 2026 names are access classes, not Elo to memorize.

Headline parameter counts are not a quality score.

Parameter counts do not help a resident decide whether to trust an answer. Mixture-of-experts vs dense. Laptop 27-billion and 30-billion models vs trillion-parameter datacenter models. Do not treat the y-axis as quality.

Judge the claims, not the fluency.
